## Why I Want to Build DebateForTruth

I want to build a place where everyone can discuss ideas rationally. That is where DebateForTruth began, and it is the direction I want to pursue as an entrepreneur.

Public debates have no shortage of positions. What is missing is room to explain the reasoning behind them. I want a view to come with evidence and sources, so readers can see the facts, reasoning, and value judgments behind it.

I want to unpack disagreements: do we have the same facts? How do we understand cause and effect? Which values matter to each of us? Answering those questions is more useful than repeating conclusions at one another.

New evidence can change a judgment. Discussion does not have to end with a winner and a loser. Acknowledging uncertainty and revising a view are forms of progress, too. Reasoned discussion should belong to everyone, including people who are less practiced at expressing themselves.

I am using AI coding assistance to build DebateForTruth into a working product with topics, viewpoints, evidence, and discussion mechanisms. I initiated the project, drive it forward, and continue to develop its features. I consider myself an idealist, and I want to turn those ideals into concrete work.

## AI Questions I Keep Coming Back To

### Storage for AI

I like digging into hard problems, including the conditions under which an NP-hard problem can be solved well enough for a practical use. My current research is in computer storage, and that is where I want to start with AI. Where models and data live, when they are loaded, and what is worth caching all affect whether AI can run on devices with limited memory and compute.

### How Could AGI Improve Itself?

I am optimistic about AGI’s long-term potential. I want to understand what kind of architecture would let an AI system learn from its results, evaluate its own changes, and keep the improvements that work. If that loop became reliable, it could speed up experimentation in science and engineering. Even if a suitable AGI architecture takes longer to find, I’m curious where today’s technology will prove useful over the next few years.

### How Will Devices Work Together as Compute Grows?

Compute still constrains edge devices today. As their capabilities grow, I want to study which workloads should run locally, which should go to nearby devices or the cloud, and what information the devices need to share. Those choices determine the practical gains of working together.
